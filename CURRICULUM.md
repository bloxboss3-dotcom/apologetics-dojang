# Apologetics Dojang — Curriculum Map

A proficiency-based course. Built against the shape used by university philosophy-of-religion syllabi (arguments for and against God's existence → the rationality of belief → problems in Christian philosophical theology) and by apologetics certificate programs (challenges → responses → disposition), with a thinking-tools prerequisite section that most apologetics curricula assume and skip.

**Proficiency target:** you can state any objection in this course well enough that its best defender would sign it, answer it without bluffing, name where your own answer is weakest, and know when to stop arguing.

---

## Pedagogy

| Mechanism | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Teach before test | Every drilled item appears first on a read screen. Nothing is assessed cold. |
| Retrieval practice | Every screen requires production, not recognition. (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) |
| Diminishing cues | Verses lose scaffolding in three stages before free recall. (Fiechter & Benjamin, 2019) |
| Spacing | Verses and key claims resurface on widening gaps — roughly 10–20% of the target retention interval. (Cepeda et al., 2008) |
| Interleaving | Units mix argument-form drills with content drills rather than blocking by topic. |
| Elaborative encoding | Each objection is anchored to one concrete image (gold in fire, a fence in a road) that acts as the retrieval handle. |
| Generation | Written answers scored live; the score determines whether the round closes. |
| Desirable difficulty | Drills are tuned to feel slightly too hard. (Bjork) |

**Mastery gate.** A unit is cleared when the boss is downed, which requires a written answer scoring ≥3.5 average on accuracy, charity, and clarity. Drills alone never clear a section.

**Checkpoint.** End of each section: three written answers, mixed units, no re-reading allowed.

## Section 1 · Ground rules
*Prerequisite for everything else. Most apologetics failures are reasoning failures, not information failures.*

1. **Arguments, not opinions** — premise, conclusion, validity vs. soundness. Why "that's just your opinion" is a category error.
2. **Three ways to reason** — deduction, induction, abduction. Most apologetics is inference to the best explanation; saying so defuses "you can't prove it."
3. **Who carries the burden** — burden attaches to claims, not to sides. Flew's presumption of atheism and the standard reply.
4. **Fallacies you'll actually meet** — straw man, genetic, equivocation, false dilemma, ad hominem, appeal to consequences.
5. **BOSS · The Muddle** — steelman drill.

*Sources: Copi & Cohen on informal logic; Flew, "The Presumption of Atheism"; Koukl, Tactics.*

## Section 2 · What we mean by "God"
*You cannot defend a term you have not defined. Half of the incoherence objections dissolve here.*

6. **The attributes** — omnipotence, omniscience, omnibenevolence, aseity, eternity, simplicity. Classical theism vs. theistic personalism.
7. **The stone paradox** — omnipotence as the power to do what is logically possible. Nonsense does not become sense by prefixing "God can."
8. **Euthyphro** — arbitrary or above him? The third option: God's own nature as the standard (Adams, Alston).
9. **Foreknowledge and freedom** — Boethius's eternal present; Molinism and middle knowledge; open theism. Knowing is not causing.
10. **BOSS · The Incoherence.**

*Sources: Aquinas, ST I; Anselm, Proslogion; Boethius, Consolation V; Plantinga, God, Freedom, and Evil; Robert Adams, Finite and Infinite Goods; Brian Davies, An Introduction to the Philosophy of Religion.*

## Section 3 · Reasons to think he's there
*Natural theology. Learn the arguments and their best objections together — an argument you can't attack you don't understand.*

11. **Contingency** — Leibniz's question; the principle of sufficient reason; the brute-fact reply.
12. **Kalam** — Craig's version; actual infinites and Hilbert's hotel; quantum and "universe from nothing" objections.
13. **Fine-tuning** — the constants; necessity, chance, or design; the multiverse reply and the Boltzmann brain problem.
14. **The moral argument** — ontology vs. epistemology (the "you don't need God to be good" confusion); Mackie's queerness argument; evolutionary debunking.
15. **The argument from reason** — Lewis's version; Plantinga's evolutionary argument against naturalism.
16. **Warrant without argument** — reformed epistemology; properly basic belief; Clifford vs. James on the ethics of belief.
17. **BOSS · The Shrug.**

*Sources: Craig, Reasonable Faith; Pruss on contingency; Robin Collins on fine-tuning; Plantinga, Warranted Christian Belief; Reppert, C.S. Lewis's Dangerous Idea; Mackie, The Miracle of Theism (the best opposition).*

## Section 4 · Reasons to doubt
*Taught at full strength. If you learn these from defenders only, you will meet them for the first time in an argument you are losing.*

18. **The problem of evil I** — logical version; the free will defense; why most philosophers regard the logical version as answered.
19. **The problem of evil II** — evidential version; Rowe's fawn; skeptical theism and Wykstra's CORNEA; Marilyn Adams on horrendous evils.
20. **Divine hiddenness** — Schellenberg's argument; responses from soul-making, divine timing, and resistance.
21. **Why people believe** — Freud, Feuerbach, and the cognitive science of religion. The genetic fallacy cuts both ways.
22. **The geography of faith** — pluralism; Hick; the "you'd be a Muslim if born in Riyadh" argument and where it turns on itself.
23. **BOSS · The Silence.**

*Sources: Rowe (1979); Wykstra (1984); Schellenberg, Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason; Hick, An Interpretation of Religion; Justin Barrett on CSR; Stump, Wandering in Darkness.*

## Section 5 · The Bible under fire
*The texts your family will actually open.*

24. **How to read an ancient text** — genre, ANE convention, accommodation, and the price each move costs you.
25. **The slavery texts** — debt servitude vs. chattel slavery; Exodus 21:16; Philemon; the trajectory argument and its limits.
26. **Manuscripts** — Ehrman's variants; how many are meaningful and viable; embarrassment of riches vs. embarrassment.
27. **The Gospels as testimony** — Bauckham on eyewitnesses; undesigned coincidences; names and geography as reliability markers.
28. **Genesis and science** — Walton's functional-origins reading; day-age and framework views; what is actually at stake doctrinally.
29. **BOSS · The Accusation** (the Canaanite conquest).
30. **BOSS · The Contradiction** (wrath, woe, and hell).

*Sources: Copan & Flannagan, Did God Really Command Genocide?; Hess on the conquest sites; Wolterstorff; Thom Stark (the strongest critic); Bauckham, Jesus and the Eyewitnesses; Wallace vs. Ehrman; Walton, The Lost World of Genesis One; Fudge and Hart on hell, opposite sides.*

## Section 6 · Jesus
*The hinge. Everything above is preamble if this fails.*

31. **Did he exist** — Tacitus, Josephus, Paul's letters; why the Christ-myth thesis stays marginal.
32. **The minimal facts** — death, burial, appearances, origin of the disciples' belief, James, Paul (Habermas and Licona).
33. **The alternatives** — hallucination, swoon, wrong tomb, legendary development, cognitive dissonance; what each explains and what it doesn't.
34. **Hume on miracles** — the argument, Earman's critique, and the Bayesian reframe.
35. **BOSS · The Legend.**

*Sources: N.T. Wright, The Resurrection of the Son of God; Licona, The Resurrection of Jesus; Ehrman, How Jesus Became God (the opposition); Earman, Hume's Abject Failure.*

## Section 7 · The conversation
*The section most programs leave out, and the one that decides whether any of the rest works at a kitchen table.*

36. **When the objection is a wound** — reading which question is actually being asked.
37. **Columbo questions** — "What do you mean by that?" "How did you come to that conclusion?" Putting a stone in the shoe rather than winning.
38. **Family, deconstruction, and losing well** — the difference between a relative and an interlocutor; what changes when you can't walk away.
39. **BOSS · The Mirror** — the final round is against your own worst habits.

*Sources: Koukl, Tactics; Volf, Exclusion and Embrace; Rogers on empathic listening; Lewis, A Grief Observed.*
